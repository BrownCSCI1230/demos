# Light attenuation

Open `index.html` directly in a browser with WebGL enabled. No build, downloads, or dependencies are required.

Two identical perspective scenes show a point light moving between heights 0.65 and 5. The left uses min(1/d², 1) to keep the attenuated light in the LDR range; the right uses min(1/(c₁+c₂d+c₃d²), 1), as in the reference slide. Presets are selected manually and remain fixed while the light moves. Pause stops light motion. Adjusting height pauses the motion. Reset restores the initial animation and coefficients.

Four cuboids sit at the corners of each ground plane, with heights 1, 2, 3, and 4 units and identical arrangements in both scenes. The fragment shader intersects camera rays with the plane and cuboids, evaluates lighting using each face normal, and casts hard shadows from the point source. Both sides use the same Lambertian cosine term, matte reflectance, source intensity, camera, and direct LDR output. Light RGB components are at most 1, attenuation is capped at 1, reflectance is at most 0.57, and the cosine and visibility terms are in [0, 1]. There is no intensity multiplier, tone mapping, exposure adjustment, or shader gamma/sRGB transfer. The normalized framebuffer converts these values directly to its display channel range. The numeric readouts show attenuation after the LDR cap and before the cosine term. The visible small sphere and halo are illustrative markers for a mathematical point source. The halo blends toward the LDR light color instead of adding unbounded energy. Grid lines change surface reflectance; no ambient term, indirect lighting, or auto-exposure is added. The all-zero polynomial denominator is interpreted as saturated attenuation (1), avoiding division by zero.

With the Quadratic only preset, the rendered scenes match at every height because both attenuation factors are capped at 1. This LDR cap intentionally departs from unbounded physical inverse-square intensity below distance 1.
